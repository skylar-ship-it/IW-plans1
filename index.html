Pre-parade — the best-case scenario
It's 2028. IW won. What had to go right to get there?

The one thing that went right: We turned the surgeon into a system. Once results stopped depending on Dr. Pulver personally, every other win became possible — WMOS, OMS360, and a category no agency could touch.

5 branches — what winning looks like
🏗️ 1 · IW — the platform
Premium infrastructure, not a marketing line item — productized, high-margin, impossible to rip out
  • Relentless "implementation infrastructure" positioning kept IW out of the lead-gen price war
  • One documented core system, configured per practice — real operating leverage, widening margins
  • Workflow integration made IW infrastructure practices depended on — high renewal rates
  • Pulver's time went into IP and credibility, not every implementation call
🔬 2 · Pulver Oral Surgery — the flagship
Pulver stopped being the bottleneck and became the credible R&D lab the whole story rests on
  • System-attributable metrics replaced "Pulver's lifetime numbers" — survived buyer scrutiny
  • Second and third surgeon hit the same outcomes — killed the "only works with Pulver" objection
  • Pulver's reputation pulled in surgeon-mentors, deepening the delivery bench
✅ 3 · West Michigan Oral Surgery — the proof of transfer
The early partner became the reference account that sold everyone else
  • A different multi-surgeon group in Grand Rapids ran the IW system and produced — the single most persuasive fact in every later pitch
  • Direct and referral volume rose together — no cannibalization
  • Conservative arch targets meant Phase-2 triggered on schedule, relationship stayed strong
  • Every prospect — including OMS360 — called WMOS before signing
🏥 4 · OMS360 — the enterprise anchor
The committee said yes for the right reasons — proof, not promises — and the pilot became the rollout template
  • Led with attributable EBITDA evidence — exactly what a PE-backed group needs to act
  • Contracted before teaching — "build it ourselves" never became the cheaper option
  • Addressed the committee and corporate economic buyer, with surgeons as champions
  • Pilot rode on OMS360's existing stack — no rebuild required
  • 2–3 practice pilot hit KPIs → full network rollout → template other DSOs asked for by name
📈 5 · Overall growth
IW scaled without breaking — delivery, capital, and bench built ahead of the demand curve
  • Credentialed surgeon-mentor pipeline — growth never capped by the founder
  • Sales gated to delivery capacity — churn stayed low, references compounded
  • Working capital sized to the deferred structure — growth never choked on cash
  • Mix of independents and DSOs removed single-customer risk
  • "IW" became shorthand for full-arch implementation infrastructure — the default, not an option
